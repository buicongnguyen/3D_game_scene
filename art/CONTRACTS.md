# Starline art contracts

Every runtime model is generated by a Blender 4.5 Python script in
`art/blender/`. This file is the single source of truth shared by the art
generators, the runtime (`src/`) and the tests (`tests/assets.test.mjs`).
If an asset cannot meet its contract, change this file in the same commit and
say why.

## Style bible (read first)

**Quality bar:** Mario Kart 8 and *A Short Hike* / *Alba* storybook quality:
chunky toy-like forms, bevelled edges, saturated glossy PBR, readable
silhouettes at game distance. That is a quality reference only, never a
source of designs (no Nintendo or other IP).

**Setting:** Japanese countryside with Meiji-era Western touches, in the
spirit of Ghibli: warm, lived-in, hand-made.

- **Kawabe (west bank):** dark timber frames, white plaster, red-brown curved
  kawara roofs, engawa porches, noren curtains, red paper lanterns, potted
  plants, bamboo fences.
- **Takamori (east hill):** white clapboard, teal and green tin/tile roofs,
  bay windows, mustard trims, striped awnings, brick chimneys, flower boxes.
- **Railway:** tomato-red engine with brass and near-black, cream and teal
  coaches, weathered stone viaduct.

**Colour:** vivid and warm, never pastel or beige-grey. Avoid pure black (use
`#1d2430`-ish) and pure white (use `#f4f1e8`-ish). Use a strong accent colour
per asset. Roof and wall colours must differ clearly between the two villages.

**Form rules (from the kit):**

1. **Silhouette first.** Exaggerate the features that identify an asset.
2. **No raw primitives.** Bevel every hard edge (2–3 segments, 3–8 % of part
   size). Curved parts get enough segments to look round at camera distance.
   Smooth shade, keeping hard edges only above about 35–40°.
3. **Materials.**

   | Surface | Roughness | Metal |
   |---|---|---|
   | Paint / plastic | 0.35–0.5 | — |
   | Wood / plaster / fabric | 0.6–0.9 | — |
   | Metal | 0.25–0.45 | 0.6–0.9 |

   Glass is dark, glossy and slightly emissive only if it is a contract glow
   material. Use at most 8 materials per asset (characters at most 10).
4. **Baked AO on everything** via `bake_ao` (strength 0.45–0.7). Grounded
   assets pass `ground=0.0` so contact shadows darken the base.
5. **Detail density:** add the small things that sell reality: nails, tile
   rows, window mullions, door handles, planks, stone courses, rivets, stitched
   seams, hair strands as chunky locks. Keep them within budget.
6. **Merge static parts** with `join_by_material`; keep contract nodes
   separate.

## Coordinates, units, pivots

- Metres. Blender Z-up; assets face **−Y** in Blender (becomes **+Z** in
  three.js); exported with `export_yup`.
- **Origin:** ground centre of the footprint (buildings, props, characters and
  animals stand on z=0). Exceptions are noted per asset.
- **Buildings:** the front door is on the −Y face, centred unless stated.
  Floor at z=0. Add a stone or timber plinth that extends down to z=−0.8 so
  terrain mismatch never shows a gap.
- **Pivot nodes** (empties or meshes the runtime animates) have identity rest
  rotation, and their origin sits on the rotation axis.

## Build layout and commands

```powershell
$B = '<path to Blender 4.5 LTS>\blender.exe'   # e.g. a portable 4.5.9 install
& $B -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python art/blender/build_<family>.py [-- --only a,b]
& $B -b --factory-startup --python art/blender/review_render.py -- public/models .tools/review/<family> <names...>
python art/blender/compose_sheet.py .tools/review/<family> .tools/review/<family>/sheet.png 4 <names...>
node --test tests/assets.test.mjs
```

- Generators: `art/blender/build_<family>.py`. They use `kit.py`, plus
  `rig.py` for rigged assets.
- Outputs: `public/models/<name>.glb`. Editable sources go in
  `art/blender/source/<family>.blend`, saved compressed.
- Review renders go under `.tools/review/` (gitignored), never `public/`.
- Portraits: `public/portraits/<name>.webp`. Item icons:
  `public/icons/<item>.webp`.

## Rigged assets (characters and animals)

Build with `rig.py`:

1. Create an armature with `build_armature`.
2. Build body parts with kit primitives.
3. Weight each part with `bind` or `bind_blend`.
4. Join and bake with `skin(..., ao=...)`.
5. Author clips with `clip`.
6. Export with `export_rigged`.

Rules:

- **Bone names use underscores, never dots** (three.js strips dots).
- Clip names are exact and case-sensitive. Looping clips must match their
  first and last pose (`clip(loop=True)` does this).
- Clips are rotations about armature axes. `-X` swings a hanging limb
  **forward** and `+X` backward; `+Z` turns left. Root motion is in place: the
  runtime moves the character, and clips only bob the hips.
- Walk cycle distance: the runtime plays Walk at 1.0× for **1.6 m/s** and Run
  at 1.0× for **4.2 m/s**. Tune stride so the feet don't slide at those speeds.

### Humanoid rig (all humans)

Bone hierarchy and exact names:

```
root (at origin)
└ hips
  ├ spine ─ chest ─ neck ─ head
  │                 ├ upperarm_L ─ forearm_L ─ hand_L
  │                 └ upperarm_R ─ forearm_R ─ hand_R ─ grip_R
  ├ thigh_L ─ shin_L ─ foot_L
  └ thigh_R ─ shin_R ─ foot_R
```

- The shoulders hang from `chest`.
- `grip_R` has no weights. It sits at the palm centre; the runtime parents
  held props (lantern, rod, hammer) to it, prop +Y (three) along the forearm.
- Optional extra bones for secondary motion: `hair_1…`, `hat`, `scarf_1…`,
  `tail_1…`.
- **Proportions:** stylised chibi-realistic with a head about 1/4.5 of height,
  big expressive eyes (sclera, iris, pupil, highlight, eyelid line), eyebrows,
  small nose, and a mouth line. Hands are mitten-plus-thumb or four chunky
  fingers. Chunky hair as sculpted locks, never a helmet cap.
- Clothes carry the colour identity: seams, pockets, buttons, cuffs, belts,
  boots with soles.
- **Body shape (`art/blender/body_shape.py`):** every builder ends with
  `normalize_height(parts, b, H, style=<STYLES key>, head=hd)`. The pass runs
  on the finished, unskinned parts: the torso gains a chest/bust, waist and
  hips (width and front/back depth around the joints' own heights, by bone
  weight, so garments, belts and straps move together), the shoulders are
  pressed onto a trapezius slope with a round deltoid, and the legs are
  stretched ankle-to-hip (×1.05–1.12) by a smooth vertical warp applied to
  every part, with the joints and the builder's head moved along. Sleeves and
  trousers also get an anatomical taper (`ARM_SHAPE`, `LEG_SHAPE` in `npcs.py`;
  legs under skirts and robes opt out with `shape=0`, or the thigh pokes
  through mid-stride). Vertex counts, bone names and clips are untouched.
  `--shape-leg`, `--shape-slope` and `--shape-torso 0` switch parts of it off
  on the Blender command line for debugging.
