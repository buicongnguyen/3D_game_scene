# Starline: the story

*星の谷 · Hoshi Valley.* A cosy mystery about a railway, two feuding villages,
a tiny fallen star, and the night everybody looked away.

## Logline

Mika, thirteen, arrives in Hoshi Valley on the last train of winter to clear
out her late grandmother's cottage, and finds a sleeping star in an old signal
lantern. To bring home the Star Train that stopped running ten years ago, she
and the star, Tamo, must relight the valley's four Star Lamps, one each season.
On the way she finds out who really put the train in danger on the night of the
flood, and has to decide how that truth gets told.

## Setting

**Hoshi Valley** is a river valley crossed by the Starline, a small mountain
railway. The river runs from the forest in the north, past the mill and the two
villages, and under a tall stone viaduct. One span of the viaduct has been
missing for ten years.

| Place | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Hoshi Station** | The west-bank terminus on a high plateau. Genzo and *Kobo*, the little red valley engine, run a short shuttle from here to the tunnel. Genzo's engine shed is further west. |
| **Sora's Signal Cottage** | On the bluff beside the viaduct's west end. The porch lamp, and Sora's chest. |
| **Kawabe** (川辺, "riverside") | The west-bank fishing village: dark timber, white plaster, red-brown roofs. The dock, the boathouse, a stack of cargo crates and a sandbar with a crab on it. |
| **Ōta Mill** | Upriver of Kawabe. Its water wheel works the drawbridge to the little island where the **Mill Lamp** stands. |
| **Rin's ferry** | Crosses from Kawabe Dock to Takamori Landing. |
| **Takamori** (高森, "high wood") | The orchard village on the east hill: white clapboard, teal roofs, Hana's bakery, the bell tower with the **Orchard Lamp**, a sheep pasture, and a peach orchard full of scarecrows. **Takamori Halt** is its station on the far side of the viaduct. |
| **The forest and the shrine** | Beyond the upper orchard gate: red maples, the shrine steps, the shrine hearth and, at the top, the **Forest Lamp**. Across the river, reached by stepping stones, is the **landslide scar**. |
| **The viaduct** | Carries the line across the river to Takamori Halt. The **Viaduct Lamp** stands at its centre, beside the broken span. |

**One year, four seasons.** The prologue is the last evening of winter (the
valley already in spring colours). Chapter One is spring, Two summer, Three
autumn and Four winter; the epilogue is the next spring. Each chapter starts in
the morning and ends at dusk, when its lamp is lit. Chapter Four covers two
days: the confession and the meeting on the first, the repair and the
star-fall on the second.

**Star-fall** (*Hoshifuri*) is the winter meteor shower. On star-fall night,
Takamori held its festival and the **Star Train** (Kobo hung with lanterns,
pulling festival coaches) carried the whole valley across the viaduct together.

**Hoshibi** ("star-fire") are fallen stars small enough to live in a lamp.
Tamo is the Viaduct Lamp's hoshibi. His spark can kindle any lamp, and he grows
brighter with each lamp that is lit.

## The four Star Lamps

The lamps are a festival tradition to visitors, but the keepers know they are a
**flood-warning relay** for the railway, read from upriver down: **Forest →
Mill → Orchard → Viaduct**. Sora's first journal page gives the rule: *a lit
lamp says all clear; a dark lamp says stop; if one lamp goes dark, the next
must go dark.*

| Lamp | Where | Keeper | On the night of the flood | Lit again |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Forest** | Top of the shrine steps | Old Kiku | Put out, as it should have been | Chapter Three, autumn |
| **Mill** | The mill island, Kawabe | Grandpa Ōta | Left burning: Ōta was drinking at the festival | Chapter One, spring |
| **Orchard** | Top of Takamori's bell tower | Hana | Left burning: Hana was selling buns at the festival | Chapter Two, summer |
| **Viaduct** | The centre of the viaduct | Sora | Put out: Sora took Tamo into her lantern | Chapter Four, winter |

Mika lights them in the order the valley opens up to her (Mill, Orchard,
Forest, Viaduct). When the last one is lit, the relay burns in river order.

## What really happened

Ten years ago, on star-fall night, rain brought a hillside down into the river
upstream. The landslide dammed the river, the dam burst, and a flood surge ran
down the valley.

1. **Old Kiku** saw it and put out the Forest Lamp.
2. The **Mill Lamp** stayed lit, because **Grandpa Ōta** was at Takamori's
   festival, drinking.
3. The **Orchard Lamp** stayed lit, because **Hana** was at the festival,
   selling buns.
4. **Sora** was at the festival too, until she heard the river. She ran back,
   put out the Viaduct Lamp and scooped its hoshibi, **Tamo**, into her brass
   signal lantern.
5. **Genzo**, driving the Star Train with forty passengers, saw the dark
   Viaduct Lamp, but the Mill and Orchard Lamps both said "clear". He decided
   the wind had blown Sora's out, and did not slow down.
6. Sora ran onto the viaduct swinging the lantern, red through its glass.
   Genzo braked so hard he tore his shoulder, and the train stopped four metres
   short as the span fell. Everyone aboard lived. Sora was still on the viaduct
   when the span went, and never walked well again.

**Afterwards,** Kawabe blamed Takamori's lamp and Takamori blamed Kawabe's.
Each was half right, and blaming the other village meant never looking at their
own empty lamp. Genzo knew the whole truth and let them argue, because it was
easier. The span was never mended, the keepers let their lamps go dark, and the
Star Train never ran again. Sora forgave Genzo "before the train stopped
rolling". She kept Tamo asleep in her lantern, and before she died she left her
journal in pieces: the relay rule with Ōta, the festival page with Hana, Kiku's
page at the Forest Lamp, and the last page, sealed for Mika, with Genzo.

**Theme:** *a light only works if someone is watching it.* There is no villain.
Everyone looked away a little, and the valley needs all of them watching again.

## Synopsis

### Prologue: The Last Train of Winter

Mika arrives at Hoshi Station on Kobo. Genzo, gruff and oddly shaken ("You have
her eyes, you know"), gives her the key to Sora's cottage on the bluff by the
viaduct. In the chest on the porch are Sora's brass signal lantern with red
glass, every letter Mika ever sent her, and a note. A little gold light tumbles
out of the lantern, snoring: Tamo, a hoshibi who remembers only a lamp, rain
and a very long nap.

Sora's letter sets the goal. The four Star Lamps went dark ten years ago and
the Star Train stopped coming; Mika should light them again before this
winter's star-fall and bring the train home, starting with the Mill Lamp in
Kawabe. And: *"don't be angry with Genzo. He has carried enough."* Tamo, sad to
hear Sora is gone, decides to help, and shows off by sparking the porch lamp.

### Chapter One · Spring: The Mill Lamp

Rin, fishing on the Kawabe dock, tells Kawabe's side: the flood was Takamori's
fault, because their lamp stayed lit. Her grandfather, **Grandpa Ōta**, turns
Mika away. The Mill Lamp is on an island, the drawbridge only comes down when
the mill wheel turns, and the wheel has been jammed for ten years, three cogs
short.

Rin has a plan: Grandpa melts for grilled trout. Mika catches three fish ("any
fish, we'll call them trout"), Rin grills them, and Ōta softens enough to send
Mika after his cogs: one in the reeds by the boathouse, one hidden on top of the
crate stack, and one held like a tiny shield by a crab on the sandbar, which
Tamo boops until it lets go. The wheel turns, the drawbridge comes down, and at
sundown Tamo lights the Mill Lamp and remembers lanterns, shouting and a very
loud river. Ōta gives Mika the first journal page (the relay rule), which he
never had the heart to read, and sends her to see what Takamori has to say for
itself. Rin's ferry is open.

### Chapter Two · Summer: The Orchard Lamp

**Hana** hands Mika a bun and the other side of the story: Kawabe's Mill Lamp
stayed lit, and Kawabe was drinking at Takamori's festival. The bell-tower key
is hers to give, but first the summer fair must be saved. Mika sends five
runaway sheep home to the pen, Tamo rings five scarecrow bells to clear the
crows, and Mika fills Hana's fair basket with five ripe peaches.

Under the basket's cloth is a bun and a note from Hana: take it to Genzo, who
used to visit every week and stopped after that night. At the station Genzo
won't take it ("tell her I don't deserve Hana's buns") and won't say what
happened: "The river happened." Hana gives Mika the key anyway. Mika rings the
tower bell; at dusk Tamo lights the Orchard Lamp and remembers the train,
bright and coming and not slowing down. Hana's page says she and Ōta were both
at the festival that night. She opens the upper orchard gate to the forest,
where nobody has gone since Old Kiku left.

### Chapter Three · Autumn: The Forest Lamp

**Kon**, the shrine fox, leads Mika up through the maples to the shrine, where
**Ōkuma** the Great Bear is asleep across the steps. Old Kiku's note on the
shrine board says the forest is still sad and the bear is fond of honey
chestnuts. Mika gathers chestnuts, mushrooms and a honeycomb (Tamo knocks down
the hive, and the bees object) and cooks at the shrine hearth.

Before feeding the bear, Tamo spots a missing hillside across the river. At the
landslide scar Mika works out what caused the flood: the hill dammed the river,
and the dam burst. Nobody caused the flood. Ōkuma eats the lot, pats Mika very
gently on the head and ambles off. At dusk the Forest Lamp is lit, and Tamo
remembers almost everything: Sora running onto the viaduct with him in her
lantern, waving it, *red, red, red*, and Genzo's face in the engine's window.
Behind the lamp, kept safe by Kiku, is page three: Kiku put her lamp out; the
rest of us did not; *"I will not write down who was driving. He knows."*

### Chapter Four · Winter: The Viaduct Lamp

Snow, and the stars fall tomorrow night. The last lamp stands on a viaduct with
a hole in it, and mending the hole will take Kawabe timber and Takamori iron.
Mika takes Tamo to Genzo's engine shed. Genzo knows the little light at once,
and tells the truth: the dark Viaduct Lamp, the two lamps that said "clear",
Sora on the tracks, forty people saved, and Sora never walking right again. He
has let Kawabe and Takamori blame each other ever since. Sora gave him the last
page of her journal before she died, sealed for Mika; he opened it and has read
it every day: *"I forgave you before the train stopped rolling. Now forgive
yourself."* Then Mika makes [the choice](#the-choice).

Mika invites Ōta and Hana to the station. That evening, on the snowy platform,
the truth comes out, and then Ōta and Hana each admit they weren't watching
their lamps either. "So… nobody's the villain?" says Rin. "That's somehow
worse." Tamo: "No! That's BETTER! It means everybody can fix it!" Ōta offers the
mill's seasoned timber, Hana the smithy's bolts. "Tomorrow."

In the morning Mika sets three new beams across the gap. Kobo won't cross until
all four lamps say "clear", so at nightfall Mika lights the Viaduct Lamp, and
the relay burns down the river: Forest, Mill, Orchard, Viaduct. Both villages
board the Star Train. From the footplate Mika lights eight trackside lanterns
as the train crosses the viaduct to Takamori Halt, and the sky falls: meteors,
fireworks and a line of lanterns glowing on the frozen river. Tamo tells Mika the Viaduct Lamp was always his
lamp, and it needs its hoshibi. "I'll keep watch. Every night. You keep watch
too, okay?" He rises into the lamp.

### Epilogue: Keep Watch

Spring again. The Star Train runs every Sunday. Kawabe and Takamori share one
festival, on the viaduct, and argue about the buns. Sora's cottage has a new
Viaduct Lamp keeper, who waves at a small gold light every night. One line
changes with Mika's choice (below). The valley is open to explore, Kobo's
Sunday service runs all the way to Takamori Halt, and finding all twelve
Fallen Stars (possible at any point in the game) reveals Sora's last letter:
*"I never lit the lamps for the train. I lit them so someone would look up."*

## The choice

At the end of Genzo's confession, Mika chooses what he should do with the truth.

| Mika says | At the meeting on the platform | In the epilogue |
|---|---|---|
| **"You have to tell them yourself."** | Genzo stands up alone: "It was my fault. Not Kawabe's. Not Takamori's. Mine." | Genzo tells the whole story to his Sunday passengers himself: "First brave thing I did in ten years. Felt like breathing." |
| **"Grandma forgave you. We'll tell them together."** | Mika opens the meeting and stands beside Genzo while he confesses, then tells them Sora forgave him, in her own hand. | Genzo saves Mika the seat up front on Sundays: "You stood by me on that platform. She'd be proud of you." |

Either way Ōta and Hana own their part, the villages make peace and the story
continues the same way. The choice changes how the confession feels, not
whether it happens.

## Playing the story

Everything is done by walking up to something and pressing **E** (**Act** on
touch): talking, opening, fitting, cooking. Tamo's spark is automatic: near a
lamp, a bell, the crab or the hive, press E and Tamo flies over and sparks it.
Items are collected by walking into them (or pressing E beside them). Mika can
swim and dive in the river; the drawbridge and Rin's ferry are the story's
routes, but swimming across works too. There are no fail states.

## Storytelling on the train

Three times, the story is told in captions across the top of the screen while
a train is moving. Captions are narration (nobody speaks them) and never more
than 120 characters. The script in Appendix A prints them where they play.

- **The arrival (Prologue).** Five captions, about four seconds each, while
  Kobo runs in from the west tunnel. They set up the mystery (the Star Train
  stopped; the lamps went dark; everybody knows why, and everybody tells it
  differently) and then Mika: she knows the valley only from her
  grandmother's letters, and she doesn't know the cottage isn't empty. They
  leave the rest to the scene that follows: the narrator names Kobo and "the
  last train of winter", Genzo recognises Mika and hands her the key.
- **The Star Train (Chapter Four).** Six captions, timed by how far along the
  line the train is, over the lanterns Mika is lighting: out of the station
  for the first time in ten years, past Sora's porch lamp onto the viaduct
  (Genzo keeps a hand near the brake), over the Kawabe timber and Takamori
  iron of the mended span and past the Viaduct Lamp, where Tamo goes quiet
  (he knows where he belongs). Then the passengers: Hana passes Ōta the buns
  and he takes three, Rin cheers the fireworks. The last caption is the
  theme: everyone, for once, is looking up.
- **The Sunday service (Epilogue).** Mika can ride Kobo from either platform.
  Five spring captions: Rin's ferry, Tamo's small light flickering back from
  the Viaduct Lamp, the four lamps all saying "clear", Ōta pretending not to
  wave. The ride runs both ways, so only the middle caption (at 42%, which is
  on the viaduct whichever way Mika is going) names a landmark.

The line from Hoshi Station (0%) passes Sora's cottage at about 10%, is on the
viaduct from about 19% to 58% (the Viaduct Lamp and the mended span at about
37 to 42%), and runs up the east bank to Takamori Halt (100%).

## Homes and keepsakes

Four front doors open in every chapter: Sora's cottage, Hana's bakery, Ōta's
mill and Genzo's station office. Each room is a portrait of its owner, and each
keeps one keepsake for the journal. Together the keepsakes show the four
friends before the flood, which the main story only tells.

| Home | The room | Keepsake |
|---|---|---|
| **Sora's cottage** | Her glasses and teacups still on the table, a shelf of letters, a pale square on the wall, her workbench and the Star Kite. | **Festival photograph:** Sora, Genzo, Ōta and Hana squashed onto Kobo's buffer beam at star-fall, the year before the flood. On the back: "Same time next year." |
| **Hana's bakery** | A brick oven, festival bunting, buns on every surface. | **Hana's bun recipe:** "Three for Ōta. He'll say he only wants one." Ten years of feuding, and she never crossed it out. |
| **Ōta's mill** | The gear train, the fishing corner, and crocks and crocks of pickled radish. | **A pair of fishing floats** carved Ō and G. Ōta and Genzo fished together; after the flood Genzo stopped coming anywhere, and Ōta has sulked over pickled radish ever since. |
| **Genzo's station** | Timetable, ledger, pendulum clock, and more pictures of Kobo than of people. | **Kobo's first ticket,** punched the day the line opened: "Genzo, age 9. I will drive this train one day." |

The photograph's "Same time next year" only hurts once you know the next
star-fall was the night of the flood. Because Mika can walk in at any point in
the story, the scenes never assume a chapter: names are read off the objects
(the back of the photograph, the recipe card, the ticket), and each scene with
Tamo in it has a Tamo-free version.

## The Star Kite

Sora's handmade, wind-up flying machine rests on the workbench in her
cottage. It appears once Tamo has joined Mika, and a note from Sora is tied to
the handlebar: *"The Star Kite. It comes when you whistle and goes where my
legs won't. Hold on tight, and don't tell Genzo."* That is an early, gentle
hint at what Genzo confesses in Chapter Four (Sora never walked right again),
and at how she kept watching over the valley anyway. From then on G (or the
Kite button) whistles the kite down to carry Mika, and lands her again.

## Valley friends

Mika can say hello (E) to eight kinds of creature: the meadow rabbits,
Takamori's hens, Mochi the Kawabe cat, the river ducks, Hana's sheep (from
Chapter Two), the forest deer, and Kon and Ōkuma (from Chapter Three). The
first hello with each plays a one- or two-line scene and fills in its page of
the journal; meeting all eight plays one more. They are small jokes, mostly
Tamo's (he has no nose, and no idea what "quack" means), and one more reason
to walk every corner of the valley.

## Writing notes

- **Tone:** warm, witty, sincere. The grief is real, but the valley is
  beautiful and alive.
- **No violence.** Tamo's spark lights lamps, rings bells and startles a crab
  and some bees. Nothing is hurt or hunted.
- **No villain.** Every character is sympathetic by the end.
- **Short lines:** at most about 135 characters per dialogue line, and 120
  per train caption. The arrival captions show for about four seconds each,
  so keep those nearer 80.
- **Scenes that can happen at any time** (homes, keepsakes, the kite, valley
  friends) must be true in every chapter and season. Tamo is with Mika only
  from the chest to the finale, so every such scene with Tamo in it has an
  `<id>_solo` version, spoken by Mika or the narrator, which the game plays
  when he isn't there.
- **Letters and notes are voiced by their writers** (Sora's letter and pages,
  Kiku's note, Hana's note in the basket). Anyone else who speaks is on screen.
- **Idle chatter rotates across every chapter,** so each chatter line must be
  true at any point in the story.
- **Where the story lives:** `src/game/story.js` (the quest engine is
  `src/game/quest.js`). After changing it, run `npm run story:md` to refresh
  the appendices below; `node scripts/story-md.mjs --check` fails if they are
  out of date.

## Appendix A · The complete script

Every line in the order the game plays it, grouped by chapter and quest step (the objective the player sees,
