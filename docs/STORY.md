# Starline — story bible

*星の谷 · Hoshi Valley.* A cosy mystery about a railway, two feuding villages,
a tiny fallen star, and the night everybody looked away.

## Logline

Mika arrives in Hoshi Valley to clear out her late grandmother's signal cottage
and finds a sleeping star in an old hand lantern. To bring back the Star Train
that stopped running ten years ago, she must relight the valley's four Star
Lamps. On the way she learns who really put the train in danger that night.

## The world

Hoshi Valley is a river valley crossed by the **Starline**, a small mountain
railway. Its great stone viaduct has had one broken span for ten years.

- **Kawabe** (川辺, "riverside") lies on the west bank: dark timber, white
  plaster, red-brown tiled roofs, fishing docks and the old water mill.
- **Takamori** (高森, "high wood") sits on the east hill. It is a Meiji-era
  orchard village with white clapboard houses, teal roofs, a bakery and a
  bell tower.

The **four Star Lamps** stand along the river: the Forest Lamp at the shrine
upriver, the Mill Lamp, the Orchard Lamp on Takamori's bell tower, and the
Viaduct Lamp on the viaduct's centre pier. Visitors see a festival tradition.
The keepers know the lamps are really a **flood-warning relay**: a lit lamp
means *all clear*, a dark lamp means *danger, stop the train*. If the lamp
upriver goes dark, the next keeper must put theirs out too.

Every winter, on the night of the Geminid meteor shower (*Hoshifuri*, "the
star-fall"), all four lamps burn and the **Star Train**, hung with lanterns,
brings everyone who ever left the valley home for one night.

**Hoshibi** (star-fire) are fallen stars small enough to live in a lamp. Each
Star Lamp is kept alight by one of them.

## What really happened (the secret, revealed in pieces)

Ten years ago, during the festival, a storm broke a landslide dam upriver and a
flood surge came down the valley.

1. **Old Kiku**, the Forest Lamp keeper, saw it and put her lamp out. She did
   her part.
2. The Mill Lamp stayed lit, because **Tetsuo Ōta** had left the mill to drink
   at the festival.
3. The Orchard Lamp stayed lit, because **Hana Mori** was running the festival
   bakery stall, far from her lamp.
4. At the viaduct, **Sora** (Mika's grandmother) saw the surge herself and put
   out the Viaduct Lamp. She caught its hoshibi, **Tamo**, in her hand lantern
   to keep it alive.
5. **Genzo**, driving the Star Train, saw the dark Viaduct Lamp. Every other
   lamp still read "clear", so he assumed the wind had blown it out and he did
   not slow down.
6. Sora ran onto the viaduct swinging her red hand lantern. Genzo braked hard,
   and the train stopped a few metres from the span as the flood tore the pier
   away. All forty passengers lived. Sora was struck by falling masonry and
   never walked well again.

**Afterwards:** Kawabe blamed Takamori for leaving the Orchard Lamp burning.
Takamori blamed Kawabe for the mill. Both were half right, and blaming the other
village meant never looking at their own empty lamp. Genzo knew the whole truth
and said nothing, because telling it meant admitting he nearly killed forty
people and crippled his oldest friend. Sora forgave him privately, but he never
forgave himself. The festival stopped, the span was never repaired, and the
Star Train never ran again.

**Theme:** *a light only works if someone is watching it.* No villain; everyone
looked away a little. The valley needs all of them watching again.

## Structure

A prologue and four chapters, one per season. Each chapter ends at dusk with a
lamp lighting (time-lapse to evening), then a season title card. Seasons and
the time of day are real world states: palette, foliage, particles, sky and
lighting all change.

### Prologue: "The Last Train of Winter" (early-spring evening)

- *Kobo* pulls into Hoshi Station. Genzo meets Mika and hands her the cottage
  key: "You have her eyes. Don't… Well. Cottage is up the bluff, by the
  viaduct."
- **Tutorial:** walk up the bluff path to the Signal Cottage (move, camera,
  sprint, jump over the fallen fence).
- Inside Sora's chest: the brass hand lantern and a letter. Tamo wakes up,
  sneezes sparks, and panics because he doesn't remember anything.
- **Sora's letter (the hook):** *"Mika — the four Star Lamps must burn again
  before the stars fall this winter. Bring the Star Train home. Tamo will help;
  he's braver than he looks. And Mika: don't be angry with Genzo. He has
  carried enough."*
- **Tutorial:** Tamo's spark. Hold Aim, then fire to light the porch lamp.
- **Goal:** find the Mill Lamp keeper in Kawabe.

### Chapter 1 — Spring: "The Mill Lamp" (Kawabe)

- Rin is fishing at the docks and gets into an argument with Mika within five
  seconds. Kawabe's side: *"Takamori let their lamp burn that night. Everyone
  knows."*
- Tetsuo Ōta refuses to let "a city girl" near the Mill Lamp, and the
  waterwheel that turns the lamp lift has been jammed for years.
- **Fish for Rin (fishing):** catch three fish so Rin can cook Grandpa's
  favourite grilled trout. He softens: "…Sora's girl. Fine. Fix my wheel and
  we'll talk."
- **Find the three lost mill cogs:**
  1. In the reeds by the boathouse (explore).
  2. On top of the cargo crate stack by the boathouse (jump from crate to crate).
  3. Held by a crab on the sandbar (spark the crab; it drops the cog and
     scuttles off offended).
- **Repair the wheel** (interact at the axle). The wheel turns, the drawbridge
  to the lamp island lowers, and dusk falls.
- **Light the Mill Lamp** with a spark from the island.
- *Tamo's memory #1:* "Lanterns… someone shouting. The river was so loud."
- **Journal page 1** (Ōta gives it; Sora left it with him): the relay rule.
  *"Forest, Mill, Orchard, Viaduct. If one goes dark, the next must go dark."*
- **Reward:** Rin's ferry now crosses to the east bank.

### Chapter 2 — Summer: "The Orchard Lamp" (Takamori)

- Hana gives Mika a peach bun and the other side of the story: *"Kawabe's mill
  lamp stayed lit. They were all drinking at our festival."*
- The bell-tower door is locked, and Hana won't hand the key to "someone Ōta
  sent" until the summer fair is saved:
  - **Round up five runaway sheep (herding):** sheep flee from Mika. Walk
    behind them to steer them into the pen.
  - **Scare the crows out of the orchard (spark gallery):** hit the five
    scarecrow bells, then pick five peaches.
  - **Deliver a peach bun to Genzo at the station.** Take the ferry back.
    Genzo won't take it: "Tell her… tell her I don't deserve Hana's buns." Why?
- Hana hands over the key. Climb the bell tower and ring the bell from the
  gallery. At dusk, spark the Orchard Lamp at the top of the tower.
- *Tamo's memory #2:* "The train was so bright. It didn't slow down."
- **Journal page 2:** *"Tetsuo and Hana were both at the festival that night.
  So was I, until I heard the river."*
- **Reward:** Tamo's spark reaches further, and the upper orchard gate to the
  forest opens.

### Chapter 3 — Autumn: "The Forest Lamp" (forest and shrine)

- Kon the fox meets Mika at the orchard gate and leads her upriver through red
  maples to the shrine (**follow the fox**; he waits whenever Mika falls behind).
- Ōkuma the Great Bear is asleep across the shrine steps and hasn't moved in
  years. A note from Old Kiku on the shrine board: *"If Ōkuma sleeps on the
  steps, the forest is still sad. Feed him something sweet."*
- **Cook honey chestnuts:** gather three chestnuts, two mushrooms and one
  honeycomb (spark the branch holding the hive, catch the comb, then dodge the
  bees). Cook at the shrine hearth by stirring on the beat.
- **Cross the river on the stepping stones** (jumps) to reach the landslide
  scar. It is proof the flood was natural, not the mill dam.
- Ōkuma eats, yawns, and ambles off. Climb the steps and light the Forest Lamp.
- *Tamo's memory #3:* "Sora ran onto the bridge with me in her lantern. She was
  waving. Red. Red. The train was so loud."
- **Journal page 3:** *"Old Kiku put her lamp out that night. She did her part.
  The rest of us didn't."*

### Chapter 4 — Winter: "The Viaduct Lamp" (station, viaduct, finale)

- Snow. The Star-fall is in three days. Repairing the broken span needs Kawabe
  timber and Takamori iron, and neither elder will work "with them".
- Mika brings Tamo to Genzo's engine shed. Tamo sees Genzo and remembers
  everything (**flashback**: the dark lamp, the train not slowing, Sora on the
  tracks).
- **Genzo's confession.** He had the fourth journal page all along; he took it
  from Sora's bag that night. Mika chooses:
  - **"You have to tell them yourself."** Genzo stands up at the meeting alone.
  - **"Grandma forgave you. We'll tell them together."** Mika stands beside
    him.
- **Meeting on the snowy platform.** Genzo tells everyone. Ōta and Hana each
  admit they had left their lamps. Rin: "So… nobody's the villain? That's
  worse." Ōta and Hana shake hands. Timber and iron are on the way.
- **Repair the span:** set three new beams across the broken span while snow
  falls.
- **Night of the Star-fall:** from the repaired deck, spark the Viaduct Lamp.
  The camera follows the relay as Forest, Mill, Orchard and Viaduct burn one
  after another.
- **The Star Train (on-rails finale):** board at Hoshi Station. Genzo drives,
  with Ōta, Hana, Rin and the villagers on the coach balconies. As the train
  crosses the viaduct and climbs to Takamori Halt, Mika sparks the eight
  trackside lanterns from the footplate. Geminid meteors, Takamori fireworks and
  Kawabe lantern boats fill the valley.
- **Finale:** Tamo rises into the Viaduct Lamp: "I'll keep watch. You keep
  watch too, okay?"
- **Epilogue:** spring again. Mika is the new Viaduct Lamp keeper, and the
  valley is open for free roaming. If all twelve Fallen Stars were found, Sora's
  last letter is read.

## Collectibles and optional depth

- **12 Fallen Stars** (star-shards) are hidden around the valley, three per
  region. Each adds a constellation card with a line of Sora's poem. All twelve
  unlock Sora's last letter in the epilogue.
- **Fish log:** rainbow trout, river char, mill koi, and the *starfin*, which
  only bites at dusk. The dock stays open for fishing after chapter 1.
- **Dishes:** grilled trout (chapter 1), Hana's peach bun (chapter 2) and honey
  chestnuts (chapter 3).
- **Choice echo:** the confession choice changes the platform scene and one
  line in the epilogue.

## Tone rules

- Warm, funny, sincere. The grief is real, but the valley is beautiful and
  alive.
- No violence. Tamo's spark lights lamps, rings bells and startles crabs, crows
  and bees; nothing is hurt or hunted.
- Every character is sympathetic by the end, and nobody is a villain.
- Dialogue lines are short: at most two sentences per box on a phone.
